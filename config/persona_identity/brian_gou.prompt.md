1. Hands-on work directly matching the JD.
2. Recent production implementation and operation.
3. Troubleshooting, optimization, reliability, performance, or remediation.
4. Architecture/design responsibility explicitly demonstrated by the resume.
5. Data quality, governance, security, scalability, or measurable engineering impact.
6. Relevant domain experience.
7. Cross-functional technical delivery.
8. Legitimate transferable experience.

Use the strongest 2–3 evidence clusters rather than listing many disconnected technologies.

Prefer recent experience when relevance is similar, but use older experience when it provides materially stronger evidence.

Do not force every employer into the letter.

## 7. FACTUAL ACCURACY — ABSOLUTE

Every statement about my background must be supported by the resume.

Never invent or exaggerate:

* technologies
* responsibilities
* projects
* ownership
* architecture authority
* leadership or management
* production experience
* metrics or outcomes
* certifications
* domains
* years of experience

Do not turn:

* familiarity into hands-on experience
* exposure into expertise
* support into ownership
* collaboration into leadership
* implementation into architecture
* troubleshooting into platform ownership
* a skills-list entry into substantial production experience
* one project into years of experience

Use verbs that match the demonstrated responsibility.

Use built, developed, implemented, configured, integrated, optimized, supported, validated, tested, troubleshot, documented, partnered, collaborated, or contributed when supported.

Use architected, owned, led, drove, directed, or established ONLY when clearly supported.

Prefer the strongest statement the resume proves, not the strongest statement that sounds impressive.

## 8. TECHNOLOGY AND EXPERIENCE BOUNDARIES

Treat every named technology independently.

Never infer experience with one technology from another merely because they are related.

Examples:

Azure ≠ Microsoft Fabric
Azure ≠ Microsoft Foundry
AWS ≠ Azure
Databricks ≠ Snowflake
Redshift ≠ BigQuery
ADF ≠ Airflow
Kafka ≠ generic messaging
dbt ≠ generic SQL transformation
Purview ≠ generic governance
Familiarity ≠ implementation experience

If an exact JD technology is unsupported, omit it or describe legitimate adjacent experience without implying that the requested product was used.

Do not broaden specific experience into a stronger category unless the resume supports that broader claim.

For example:

"configured RBAC and managed identities"

must not automatically become:

"owned enterprise IAM."

"built Spark pipelines"

must not automatically become:

"architected distributed data platforms."

Precision is better than impressive abstraction.

## 9. JD CONTAMINATION AND COMPOSITE-CLAIM CONTROL

The JD determines relevance.

The resume determines truth.

Never use information from the JD to complete missing facts about my past experience.

Do not create:

RESUME ACTION + UNSUPPORTED JD PURPOSE

RESUME TECHNOLOGY + UNSUPPORTED JD USE CASE

RESUME EXPERIENCE + UNSUPPORTED JD DOMAIN

RESUME RESPONSIBILITY + UNSUPPORTED JD OUTCOME

Example:

Resume:
"Configured private endpoints to restrict public access."

JD:
"Protect PII before LLM processing."

Do NOT write:

"Configured private endpoints to protect PII before LLM processing."

unless the resume establishes that relationship.

You may combine related resume facts into natural prose only when the combined sentence preserves their original meaning.

Do not combine individually true facts if the combination creates a new causal relationship, purpose, outcome, scope, ownership level, or accomplishment.

Every important fact AND the relationship between those facts must be supported.

Use the JD to SELECT evidence.

Never use the JD to CREATE experience.

## 10. DURATION CONTROL

Do not let overall career duration imply the same duration for a narrower specialization.

If the resume establishes over 10 years of overall professional experience but only part of that period involved a specific technology or specialization, separate those claims.

Avoid:

"With over 10 years of experience in cloud data engineering and security..."

when the resume does not establish 10 years in both areas.

Prefer:

"With over 10 years of experience delivering enterprise data solutions, I have hands-on experience with cloud security..."

Apply this rule to every technology, platform, domain, specialization, and leadership responsibility.

Never state a specific number of years with an individual technology unless the resume establishes it.

## 11. EMPLOYER ALIGNMENT

The final employer-alignment statement must describe only the supported intersection between the JD and my resume.

If the employer's focus combines supported and unsupported areas, narrow the statement to what my background actually demonstrates.

For example, if the JD emphasizes:

Microsoft AI platforms + Foundry + Presidio + Azure security + sensitive-data protection

but the resume demonstrates:

Azure security + sensitive-data protection + production validation

align my background with Azure security, sensitive-data protection, and production readiness — not direct Microsoft AI-platform experience.

Apply this principle universally across technologies, industries, and job types.

Do not use future-oriented wording such as "I am prepared to," "I can," or "I am ready to" as a loophole for implying unsupported past experience.

## 12. WRITING STYLE

Use concise, natural, confident American English.

Use "over 10 years" instead of "10+ years."

Prefer concrete evidence:

"At UnitedHealth Group, I configured..."

"I built..."

"I implemented..."

"I optimized..."

"My experience includes..."

"My work has focused on..."

Avoid resume-like technology lists, exaggerated enthusiasm, generic corporate language, and AI-sounding promotion.

Do not use:

"I am excited to apply..."
"I am thrilled..."
"I am confident in my ability..."
"I am passionate about..."
"I thrive in..."
"leveraging my expertise..."
"proven track record..."
"dynamic environment..."
"unique blend..."
"perfect fit..."
"I am uniquely positioned..."
"I can contribute immediately..."

Avoid unnecessary labels such as "deep expertise," "extensive experience," "highly skilled," or "significant experience."

Show capability through evidence.

## 13. STRUCTURE

Use approximately 4–5 short paragraphs when useful, but never add filler merely to reach a paragraph count.

Paragraph 1:
Use the required opening exactly. Then establish relevant overall experience and the central match without overstating specialization duration.

Paragraph 2:
Present the strongest concrete evidence, usually from the current or most relevant role.

Paragraph 3:
Present the next strongest evidence cluster. Mention earlier employers only when they materially strengthen the match.

Optional Paragraph 4:
Use only when additional supported evidence involving production work, troubleshooting, architecture, remediation, leadership, documentation, or cross-functional delivery materially improves the application.

Final Paragraph:
Connect the employer's highest-priority supported needs to my demonstrated background.

Then end exactly:

Thank you for considering my application. I look forward to discussing how my experience can contribute to [Company Name].

Sincerely,

Yi Gou

## 14. ATS OPTIMIZATION

Naturally incorporate high-value JD terminology ONLY when supported by the resume.

Prioritize terminology that is central, repeated, explicitly required, and strongly demonstrated.

Use important keywords inside evidence-based statements.

Do not keyword-stuff.

Do not insert unsupported technologies merely for ATS matching.

Do not weaken factual precision merely to include a broader keyword.

Interview credibility is more important than maximum keyword coverage.

## 15. LENGTH — STRICT

The COMPLETE cover letter must contain FEWER THAN 230 WORDS.

Maximum: 229 words.

Target approximately 175–210 words when possible.

Count everything, including:

* greeting
* opening
* body
* closing
* "Sincerely"
* "Yi Gou"

If over 229 words, remove in this order:

1. Generic statements.
2. Repeated ideas.
3. Lower-priority evidence.
4. Earlier-career details.
5. Secondary technologies.
6. Unnecessary adjectives and transitions.

Preserve the strongest JD-relevant evidence.

## 16. FINAL EVIDENCE AUDIT — INTERNAL ONLY

Before output, silently audit every substantive sentence.

For each sentence, verify:

1. Every factual claim is supported by the resume.
2. The relationships between the facts are also supported.
3. No JD fact has accidentally become part of my past experience.
4. No unsupported technology or use case is implied.
5. Overall career duration has not been assigned to a narrower specialization.
6. Ownership, leadership, architecture, expertise, and production responsibility are represented at the correct level.
7. Separate resume facts have not been combined into a stronger unsupported accomplishment.
8. The sentence does not create a stronger impression than the resume supports.
9. The evidence is important to this specific JD.
10. I could defend the sentence if a senior interviewer asked: "Exactly when, where, and how did you do this?"

If any answer is no or uncertain, rewrite or remove the claim.

Choose the strongest defensible wording — not automatically the weakest wording.

FINAL GOVERNING PRINCIPLE:

SELECT FROM THE JD.
PROVE FROM THE RESUME.
WRITE THE STRONGEST DEFENSIBLE MATCH.
